# Usual presence

Presence simulation **drawn from your history**. The plugin records when your lights are switched on and your shutters opened, draws the trends (earliest, latest, on average), the exceptions and what never happens, then replays them while you are away (**Simulation On**), with a random change every day. The simulation is a **dry run by default**: it decides and logs everything, without sending anything to the devices.

## Privacy

Everything is computed on the box. The plugin makes **no network call** (checked by `tests/selftest.php`), uses no online service and no "AI". It changes no device, command or scenario of the box, except its own device (the widget), the followed lamps and shutters during **Simulation On** once **Really send to the devices** is ticked (never in a dry run) and, **only when you ask**, the history setting of the followed commands (see the Guide, question 4). State changes are kept in a table of the Jeedom database (`presencehabituelle_event`) and computed trends in `data/habitudes.json`, the plan in `data/plan.json` and its images in `data/plan_*.jpg`, the simulation log (7 days) in `data/simulation.log.json` (not reachable from the Web), the calibration (real and simulated per room, hour by hour) in the plugin configuration. Only administrators can see them. Like the rest of the database, they are part of the **Jeedom backups**, including a cloud backup if you set one up. The **Erase all** button (Trends tab) deletes the records, the trends, the calibration and the simulation log (the plan is erased by deleting its floors); they are also erased when the plugin is reinstalled after being removed. **Before uninstalling, click Erase all**, and **Put the history back as before** if you enabled the history from the Guide: uninstalling alone leaves the table in the database and erases the previous history setting, with no way back.

## Guide (four questions)

The plugin knows nothing about your house. The **Guide** tab reads your devices and their history, then asks you four questions; your answers fill in the "Followed commands" and prepare the future simulation.

1. **Is it a lamp or a shutter of the house?** For each light, shutter, plug (one per device) or command flagged by the discovery: **Yes, follow it**, **No** (a fridge plug, a status LED), **Duplicate of…** (the same lamp seen by another command: the chosen one is kept) or **Group of lamps** (a command that switches several lamps already listed). **Identify by switching on or opening** shows what changes, with its value, while you switch a lamp on or off, open or close a shutter: if several commands change together (within 5 seconds), keep the one that stands for it, the others become its duplicates. Any binary or typed command that changes shows up, even if it was not offered (a virtual, the second command of a device). A command cannot be the duplicate of a duplicate; if the kept command gets another answer, its duplicates follow it (duplicate) or are "to review". For a followed shutter, choose **open = high value** (the most common) or **open = 0 (low value)**: open it while identifying, the value shown is the open one. Each row shows its source (Virtual, Zigbee2MQTT…) and its history: **📈 N values / D d** (kept over the last 90 days; green from 30 values over 30 days), click to see the Jeedom chart; "not logged" otherwise. **⚙ Scenario**: a scenario that runs a command of the device (switch on, open…), with the number of its actions noted in its log; click to read the log. It only shows what the scenario does, not manual actions (which make your habits), nor an order sent from a code block, and Jeedom keeps only its last lines: a hint for choosing, not a history. The filters (type, source, history) help you choose, but nothing is ticked for you. A virtual that copies another command is proposed as "Duplicate of" the original. For a **Group of lamps**, tick in "Lamps…" the ones it switches on: this list will be used by the simulation (the analysis does not use it yet).
2. **Where are they?** (optional) The followed commands not yet placed: a click opens the Plan tab to place them.
3. **Any rules?** (optional) Per lamp or shutter: **Never simulate it** (not even while you are away), or time ranges: "may switch on" (only within these ranges) or "never on" (never within these); for a shutter, "may open" or "never open", every day, on weekdays or at the weekend (as set in the configuration). A "never on" range can be ticked **sauf exception** (except now and then): about one day in 30, drawn at random, it does not apply (a late evening stays possible, rarely). From 23:00 to 07:00 crosses midnight; from 00:00 to 00:00 = the whole day.
   **Presence sensors** (optional): a motion or presence sensor that switches lamps on, linked to Jeedom or not (a sensor that drives its lamps directly). **＋ Jeedom sensor…** lists the Jeedom presence sensors not yet declared, of any brand (Presence generic type, otherwise from the name: presence, motion, occupancy, PIR…; settings and opening, smoke, water or gas sensors are left out): choosing one fills in the name, the command and the followed lamps of the same Jeedom room, to check with **Lamps…**; **＋ sensor** adds one not linked. Give it a name, its Jeedom command if there is one, and tick the lamps it switches on. Two lamps of the same sensor always come on together (for instance the hall and staircase ceiling lights): the discovery no longer proposes them as duplicates, the Trends mark them "on sensor", and they will be simulated together, in brief switch-ons, in a coming version (for now the list is only kept). A lamp whose switch-ons are almost all quick is flagged "sensor?": declare its sensor here.
4. **Not enough data yet**: the followed commands recorded for less than 10 days (a non-historized command only from the time it is saved). Wait, or give them a "may switch on" range. Below the list, the followed commands **without usable Jeedom history** (not historized or smoothed): **Enable their history…** ticks "Historize" and sets smoothing to "None" (non-binary command), after confirmation and on these commands only; their previous setting is kept (`data/historiser_avant.json`) and **Put the history back as before** restores it (except on a command you have set differently since: it is left as is and reported). "Or do it by hand" gives the steps in Jeedom. This is the only time the plugin changes a command of another device. The command is saved as from its configuration page: the plugin that manages it is told and may react in its own way.

The **summary** says, for each followed command: habits (10 days of records or more), ranges given, never simulated, or to complete. When nothing is left to complete: "Simulation can be proposed" (a room without enough days follows provisional times, see Simulation). **Remettre le Guide à zéro** (reset the Guide) clears the answers, rules and sensors of the Guide (nothing is saved before **Save**). **Save** keeps the answers, the rules, the choice of commands and the plan together (a range without a time is refused); **Back to the previous choice** brings them back too.

## Trends

The Trends tab shows what the plugin draws from your history; the simulation uses it.

1. **Followed commands**: state commands are offered according to their generic type.
   - Lights (`LIGHT_STATE`) and shutters (`FLAP_STATE`): ticked.
   - Plugs and media: offered unticked (a plug may power a fridge).
   - Sensors and others: hidden, not recommended (they say nothing about your gestures).
   - A command without a generic type is not offered by default: set its type, or tick "Show other commands".
2. **Groups**: each lamp is analysed alone; give several lamps the same group to analyse them together (a room), or use "Group by Jeedom object"; **Une lampe par groupe** (one lamp per group) clears every group (each lamp alone), then save.
3. **History**: when saving, the state changes of the last 90 days are imported from the Jeedom history (if the command is historized). A command that is not historized is recorded from the time it is saved. After a few hours (2 h by default, "archiving" setting), Jeedom replaces numeric values with an hourly average: when the lamp went on is lost. For such a command (flagged "smoothed history"), only the last hours are imported; set its smoothing to "None" (command configuration, History tab) so that the whole history serves in future. A binary command stays exact. The state at the start of the period (lamp already on, shutter already closed) is imported too.
   A command added later is imported the same way. Only one state command per device and per type is offered ticked.
4. **Absences**: a binary or numeric command that is true while you are away (a text "Away" mode will not do: use a binary virtual) and periods entered by hand. Days touched are not counted, otherwise the plugin would draw the trends of the empty house — or an old simulation. Its history is imported over the whole period (90 days by default); a smoothed numeric command is refused (set its smoothing to "None" or use a binary virtual). When the absence command is changed, the absences of the previous one are kept. Days when the plugin was disabled, or the box stopped for over 3 hours, are not counted either (nothing was recorded).
5. **Shutters of any brand**: a shutter is followed through its state command (position from 0 to 100, binary, or text: `open` / `closed`, `up` / `down`, `ouvert` / `fermé`; `stop`, `my` or "moving" are ignored). The direction is set per shutter in the Guide (some brands give 0 = open). Without a generic type, the discovery recognises it by its name (shutter, roller, blind, volet, store…). A shutter **without state feedback** (Somfy RTS, radio without feedback) says nothing about your habits: if it keeps the same value for 3 days, the Guide flags it; its habits cannot be drawn, give it "may open" ranges. A supposed state (RFXcom, a virtual updated by your scenarios) works like a real one. A shutter with no state command at all does not show up yet: it will come with the simulation, which acts through the action commands.
6. **Discover**: the plugin reads the last 30 days of history and makes proposals, ticked or not, which you confirm. It knows nothing about your house: it relies on generic types, names and what the history shows.
   - **Duplicates**: two commands that switch together (within 5 seconds) at least 9 times out of 10 are one lamp seen twice (for example by the device's plugin and by a gateway's), or two lamps always switched together. The historized one is kept; the other is unticked, marked "duplicate of …".
   - **Groups**: a command that is on only when others are, and switches with them (a Hue group, "Whole living room"), is unticked and marked "group of …": its lamps are enough.
   - **Lights without a generic type**: a binary command whose name or device suggests a lamp (lampe, applique, plafonnier, light…) is shown as a light, unticked, "generic type to fill in?".
   - **Days without any light**: possible absences, proposed unticked (a day without light can also be a summer day); tick those when the house was empty to add them to the periods not to analyse.
   - **Smoothed history**: a lamp whose history is smoothed (beyond a few hours Jeedom keeps only an hourly average) allows no comparison; set its smoothing to "None" or watch it for 3 days.
   - **Possible absence command**: an armed alarm or a command whose name speaks of absence, holidays or simulation (binary, or numeric not smoothed), proposed unticked.
   - **Automatic lights?**: a lamp switched on nearly every day at the same time (within 5 minutes) or at sunset (within 10 minutes, from the box's location) is only flagged: untick it yourself if a program switches it on.
   - **Without history**: nothing to compare. **Watch for 3 days** records their changes for 3 days (without analysing anything); then run the discovery again. Records are kept 7 days after the end of the watch; then those of the commands you did not tick are deleted.

   **Apply to the list** carries the ticked proposals over; nothing is saved before **Save**. **Back to the previous choice** reloads into the list the choice from before the last save (commands, groups, absence command, periods); save to go back to it. Before saving a choice that removes commands, the plugin asks for confirmation: the records of a removed command are deleted and do not come back with the previous choice; only the Jeedom history is imported again (usually 90 days, a few hours if smoothed).

   Periods when the plugin itself simulates presence (Simulation On) are excluded automatically, like its stops: it does not draw its trends from its own simulation.

The **Trends** tab shows, for each lamp or group:
- the percentage of days it is on during each hour, on weekdays and at the weekend;
- **what never happens**: the hours it has never been on;
- **exceptions**: days it was on at an hour when it is on at most 10 % of days;
- how long it stays on: average, median, half of the switch-ons between two values, shortest and longest, the share of passes shorter than 3 minutes, and unusual durations;
- lamps switched on together.

In the summary, "mostly on" = at least one day in four; for a room on a sensor, whose brief passes touch almost every hour, at least one day in four and at least half the days of its most frequent hour.

A row (weekdays or weekend) taken from fewer than 10 days is marked "to be confirmed".

A switch-on longer than 18 hours (adjustable) is taken as a missed switch-off (box stopped, bridge offline): it is discarded, and the days it touches do not count for its group (or the lamp alone).

For each shutter, on weekdays and at the weekend: morning opening and evening closing time (median, average, earliest, latest), the share of nights it is closed ("never" or "always" when so) and exceptions. An exception is a day far from the middle half of the values: by more than 30 minutes and by more than one and a half times the spread of that half (whichever is larger, Tukey's rule); at least 4 values are needed. A time taken from fewer than 10 values, or a share of nights taken from fewer than 10 nights, is marked "to be confirmed", separately for weekdays and the weekend. A morning opening is only noted if the shutter was closed at 04:00: reopening it after closing it against the heat is not getting up. A night (16:00 → 04:00) touched by an absence is not counted.

The day runs from 04:00 to 04:00, for lights as for shutters. By default the weekend runs from Friday 16:00 to Monday 04:00: Friday to Sunday evenings (16:00-04:00) and Saturday and Sunday mornings (04:00-16:00); an hour between 00:00 and 04:00 counts for the previous evening. It is set in the plugin configuration (**Weekend starts** / **ends**, day and time), for the analysis as for the rules; then click **Recompute**.

It is recomputed every night; changes older than 90 days are deleted (adjustable in the plugin configuration), to follow the season.

## Configuration and widget

At the top of the page: **Configuration** opens the plugin configuration (history import, retention, longest switch-on, weekend, and the **Simulation** part: real sending, permanent dry run, rooms lit at once, visitor, light sensor, rooms without a window; and the **Recalage** (calibration) part: automatic calibration, tolerances, see Calibration). **Widget**: the plugin has a device of its own, "Usual presence" ("Usual presence (plugin)" if another device without an object already has that name); choose its object (the dashboard room where it shows) and whether it is visible, **Advanced configuration** opens the usual Jeedom window. The widget shows the state of the simulation (stopped, dry run, running), the **Simulation On** / **Simulation Off** commands (administrators and scenarios only, like Recompute: an alarm scenario can start it), the date of the trends, the number of followed commands, the **Habitudes changées** (habits changed) info command (1 when calibration reports it, see Calibration), and a **Recompute** button (administrators and scenarios only, refused with a message less than a minute after the last computation, including a save or the nightly computation; a call through the API without an administrator session is refused, as is a scenario run in synchronous mode through the API). With the **Show the rooms usually lit** box, unticked by default, it also shows the rooms usually on now (at least one day in two at this hour, weekday or weekend, confirmed trends only): **beware, they hint at when the house is empty**, and any user who sees the widget sees them (limited accounts, apps, API). It is updated every hour and at each computation. If deleted, it only comes back by clicking **Widget** again.

## Test report

The **Rapport de test** (test report) button at the top of the page shows a text to copy for forum testing: versions (plugin, Jeedom, PHP, Debian, hardware, UTC offset, latitude rounded to 10°), and for each **kind** of followed command (type, plugin, generic type, subtype, recorded values) their number and the **action commands** the simulation would send (on / off, open / close: `AUCUNE` = the simulation could not act on these devices). Then the number of rooms, shutters, detectors, the plan, the light sensor, the calibration state room by room (gaps in %) and the last 5 errors of the log. It holds no device name and no time (a single lamp outside the plan becomes « lampe seule n », log lines lose their date, names and ids); your room and group names remain: **read it before pasting**. Nothing is sent: it is only shown on the page.

## House plan (optional)

The **Plan** tab draws the house, one plan per floor:

1. **＋ Floor**: name it ("Ground floor", "First floor"…); the arrows change the order. **Image…** uploads the floor plan: a photo of a paper plan, a screenshot, a software export (PNG, JPEG…). It is reduced to 2,000 pixels and kept on the box; without an image, you draw on a grid.
2. **Place**: click a light or a shutter under "Not on the plan", then the spot on the plan. Drag a marker to move it; the **Delete** tool sends it back to the list. Presence sensors linked to Jeedom (Guide) can be placed too (purple marker, walking icon); they do not count in the trends. A marker placed outside the room given by its Jeedom room or its name ("Bathroom 1st" placed on the ground floor, staircase lamp in the next room) gets an orange dashed outline and its tooltip says why: it is only flagged, it is allowed. If you already have a Jeedom design with your lights placed, **Take the positions from a design** reuses them (centre of each widget, in proportion to the design: use the design's background as the floor image).
3. **Room**: click the corners, then "Finish the shape", and name it. The lamps of a room are analysed together, like a group; a group typed under "Followed commands" takes precedence. Two rooms with the same name form a single group, separate from a typed group of the same name.
4. **Passage**: click at least two points (a door, a corridor, the top of a staircase; more than two for a bent line), then "Finish the shape", and choose the two linked rooms and the type; for a staircase, the second room may be on another floor. The plugin counts the **sequences**: the times a light of the second room turns on less than 3 minutes after a light of the first one turns off (and the other way round). They will be used for the simulation route.
5. **Zone to exclude**: a part of the house you want neither analysed nor simulated (a guest room, a rented flat): the commands placed inside are ignored.
6. **Edit**: with the Move tool, click a room, a passage or a zone: a panel offers Rename / Edit / Delete and orange corners to drag; ✎ and 🗑 in the lists do the same. **Undo** undoes the last change not yet saved. The **Jeedom rooms** column links each Jeedom room to a plan room (found from the name, or chosen); **Place from the Jeedom rooms** puts the lights and shutters not yet placed there. Limits: 20 floors, 200 corners per shape, image of 3 MB at most once reduced.

**Save the plan** keeps everything and recomputes the trends. Each marker shows the trend at the chosen hour ("Trend at", on weekdays or at the weekend): the fuller it is, the more often the lamp is on at that hour; for a shutter, full if it is usually open. Hover over it (or tap it, on a tablet) for details. If the plan was saved from another tab in the meantime, saving is refused: reload the page.

## Simulation

**Simulation** tab, or the **Simulation On** / **Simulation Off** commands of the widget (an alarm or absence scenario can run them). Every 5 minutes, for each room (group of lamps):

- **From its trends** when it has enough counted days (10, weekdays and weekends separately, confirmed trend); otherwise from **provisional times** for its room type, recognised from its name and the Jeedom room of its lamps (kitchen, living room, bedroom, bathroom, passage, office, other): for instance a living room from 18:00 to 23:00 on weekdays. Each room moves to its trends on its own, as soon as it has enough days; the tab says which follows what.
- A room switches on at random according to the share of days it is on at this hour, **only in the dusk** (light sensor, otherwise the sun), for a duration drawn from the distribution of the durations recorded over the calibration days, long evenings included (at least 20 switch-ons; otherwise between the quarter and three quarters of the recorded durations, and with no record, the provisional durations of the room type); a quick passage (corridor, sensor, under 3 minutes) is switched off at the next pass, 5 minutes later. Lamps usually switched on together (scenes) go on together, otherwise one lamp of the room. When it reaches an hour when it has never been on, the room switches off, except about one day in 30: the exception (forgotten lamp, late evening) stays possible, but rare.
- Each day (at 04:00) draws a **shift** of ±45 minutes and an activity of 70 to 130 %: no two days look alike.
- The **Guide rules** are followed (never simulated, allowed or forbidden ranges); at most **3 rooms** lit at the same time (adjustable).
- **Shutters**: opened and closed between the quarter and three quarters of the recorded times, otherwise 30 to 60 minutes after sunrise (not before 07:00, 08:30 at weekends) and 20 to 50 minutes after sunset (not after 22:30); a shutter usually never closed at night stays open. The simulation only opens what it closed, and only in the morning (before 16:00, even with a later "may" range); it does not close a shutter already closed (it stays yours), nor a shutter it could not open again. A shutter taken out of the simulation ("never simulated") that it had closed is opened the next morning, at 08:00.
- **Someone** switches a followed lamp on or off (visitor, neighbour): the room is left alone for 60 minutes (adjustable), including a lamp already on when Simulation On starts. With real sending only: in a dry run, the simulation does not read the lamps. A lamp still on within 10 minutes of a "switch off" of the simulation is taken for a lost order: it is sent again.
- A lamp without a command to switch it off is never switched on (the log says so). A room taken out of the simulation (command unticked, Guide) is switched off.
- **Simulation Off** switches off what the simulation switched on; shutters stay as they are. Unticking **Really send** during Simulation On does the same, then the simulation goes on as a dry run; ticking it makes it start again from scratch (nothing of the dry run is taken for the state of the house).

**Dry run** (default): nothing is sent, the **log** (7 days, the last 24 h in the tab) says each decision, from what and why. **Dry-run day** replays the next 24 hours at once in memory (sun only), as bands per room. The **Dry run even without Simulation On** box runs the trial all the time, at home. Once you have read a few days, tick **Really send to the devices**: the simulation looks, in the device of each followed lamp or shutter, for the action command linked to the followed info, otherwise the only one with the expected generic type or name (On / Off, Up / Down, otherwise a slider); if it finds none, or two, nothing is sent and the log says so.

A **Simulation On** period is excluded from the analysis, like an absence: the trends stay yours.

Not yet: paths from one room to another, checking that a shutter really moved, a preset for the randomness.

## Calibration

The simulation draws its switch-ons from the trends, but chance, dusk and the rules change the total: a room may end up lit twice as much, or half as much, as in real life. **Calibration** checks this and corrects it. It is optional: the **Recalage automatique** (automatic calibration) box of the plugin configuration, unticked by default. Unticked, nothing runs and the simulation follows the trends as they are. (The labels of this part of the interface are in French only for now.)

**How it compares.** The simulation is replayed **in memory** over the last 28 real days counted (absences, Simulation On and missed switch-offs excluded), with the sun of those days (not the light sensor) and always the same randomness: nothing is sent or kept, only the result is noted. For each room recorded for at least 10 days that the simulation is allowed to light, real and simulated are compared: hours lit per day, switch-ons per day, average duration, and the share of days lit in each hour.

**What it corrects.** Two settings per room, between ÷ 3 and × 3:

- the **frequency**: the chance of switching on at each pass (not of switching off: the end always follows the trends);
- the **duration** drawn for each switch-on.

An hour when the room is never lit stays so. A room is within tolerance when its hours lit are within ±15 % and its switch-ons within ±25 % of real life (adjustable, **Tolérances** in the configuration), or within 6 minutes and 0.1 switch-on per day (a room almost never lit). Each night, after the trends are computed, up to 5 rounds: replay, compare, correct. Calibration stops when every room is within tolerance, or **as best it can** when a setting reaches its limit (a night room lit too rarely, even × 3) or after 20 rounds in all.

**Once calibrated**, no more replay. Each night when one more day has been counted (not during an absence), a light check compares the **last 14 days** with the calibration reference, on **switch-ons only**: hours lit follow the season. A room out of tolerance on **3 checks in a row** sets the state to **Habitudes changées** (habits changed): a message in the Jeedom message centre, and the widget's **Habitudes changées** info command set to 1 (a scenario can watch it). Nothing is corrected on its own: restart the calibration. A room added, removed or changed (lamps, Guide rules, trends instead of provisional hours) restarts calibration by itself.

**The Calage (calibration) tab** shows:

- the state: Désactivé (off), Calage en cours (running, number of rounds), Calée (calibrated) or Habitudes changées (since when, which rooms);
- per room, real → simulated: hours per day, switch-ons per day (gap in %, green within tolerance, orange up to twice, red beyond), average duration, settings (frequency × duration), and two 24 h bands, real on top and simulated below. Green dot: within tolerance; orange: as best it can; red: not yet. A ⚠ flags a room out of tolerance at the last check;
- the **shutters** (weekdays): median time of closing in the evening and opening in the morning, real → simulated, gap green up to 20 minutes (adjustable). For checking only: shutters are not adjusted.

**Relancer le calage** (restart calibration) runs a calibration at once (up to 5 rounds, a few tens of seconds), then resumes it each night until it is done. **Remettre les réglages d'origine** (back to original settings) resets the settings to × 1, then restarts. Unticking automatic calibration gives the simulation back its original settings; the settings found are kept for next time.

## Light sensor

Without a sensor, dusk comes from the sun (sunrise, sunset, civil twilight, from the coordinates of the Jeedom configuration). An outdoor sensor **may** help: on grey days, lights often go on earlier, which the sun cannot see. It is not guaranteed though: depending on where it is (shade in the afternoon, a lamp nearby) and on your habits, it can do worse than the sun alone. Try it with the comparison button (below) before keeping it. Dusk is **the darker** of the two: the sensor brings dusk forward on grey days, the sun keeps the night (a sensor lit at night or failing cannot make it day). Plugin configuration: its command id (`1234` or `#1234#`) and two thresholds (night below 350 lux, day above 1000 by default; smoothed value, a cloud does not light the house again). **Backup** (optional): a second sensor, used when the first one has measured nothing for 2 hours (battery, failure; 12 hours if its last measure was a night one and the sun is not up: many sensors only report changes); with no fresh sensor, back to the sun. An indoor sensor gets less light: it needs lower thresholds (for example 50 and 120 lux). For each sensor, an optional **lamp**: the state (info command, not a power reading that never drops to 0) of the one that lights it (hall lamp near a detector); while it is on, the sensor is set aside (backup, then sun), otherwise the simulation would light itself up. Rooms without a window (cellar, WC) can be lit even by day. To find out whether the sensor beats the sun at your home: Calibration tab, **Compare with and without sensor** (the same days replayed and recalibrated twice, the sensor as its history measured it; so it must be historized). The table compares totals room by room; the **Afternoon lighting, day by day** line (minutes lit from 1 pm to sunset) tells whether the simulation lights more on the days you did (1 = follows reality day by day, 0 = unrelated): that is where a sensor is judged.
